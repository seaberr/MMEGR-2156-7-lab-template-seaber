## Part 1 Define the problem and rough prototype
Goal: Select a real-world problem that you, a friend, or a family member personally experience and refine your prototype design to address it. The goal of this week is to identify a meaningful challenge that can be solved through manufacturing and to develop the first iteration of your prototype using research, modeling, and feedback.

### Objective: Choose a problem that can be addressed with 3D printing and develop an initial design concept.

Tasks
Research
### Spiral Design Process:
Research the Spiral Design Process and describe the attributes of each category (e.g., planning, risk analysis, engineering, evaluation).
Explain how this process can guide your own project iteration and improvement.
FDM anisotropic:
Investigate how the Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) process inherently produces anisotropic parts. Document how this anisotropy may influence the performance and design of your prototype. Additionally, explore strategies to either leverage this behavior to your advantage or mitigate its limitations through design decisions, material selection, or process parameters.

### SLA Shrinkage:
Research shrinkage behavior and dimensional accuracy in SLA resin prints, and document how this may affect your prototype’s design and assembly. Include key findings in your portfolio (e.g., typical shrinkage percentages, strategies for compensation, material differences).
Laser Cut kerf:
Investigate methods for compensating for laser cutting kerf and document how these strategies influence your prototype’s design, tolerances, and assembly. Consider how kerf impacts fit, joint performance, and overall dimensional accuracy, and how design choices can account for these effects.

### Problem Definition
Identify and describe in detail several problems (3 minimum) you find interesting or worth solving.
Use clear headers and full sentences in your documentation.
Problem Selection
Select one problem that can be addressed using manufacturing processes used in class.
Explain how and why you want to solve this problem.
### Concept Development
Generate a sketch or rough model of your overall design.
Create a 3D model of your prototype based on feedback from peers.
Ensure your design is feasible for manufacturing (consider clearances, tolerances, and material properties).
### Testing Critical Features
Print or cut test pieces of challenging features (e.g., snap fits, press fits, or interlocking joints) to verify tolerances.
Video Presentation
Create a 30-second video explaining the selected problem in detail.
### Documentation
Record your full process in your portfolio, including notes, research, images, and early models.
Presentation
Present your problem and proposed solution to the class next week for feedback.
